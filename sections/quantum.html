1 · The fundamentals, in plain English

Qubit & superposition

A qubit is a two-level quantum system. Its state is a|0⟩ + b|1⟩, and measuring it returns 0 or 1 with probabilities |a|² and |b|². n qubits carry 2ⁿ amplitudes.

This is not "trying every answer at once." You read out only n classical bits. Useful algorithms use interference to boost the amplitudes of correct answers, so any speedup comes from structure in the problem.

Entanglement & gates

Entangled states can't be written as a product of single-qubit states. They give correlations with no classical analogue, and algorithms consume them as a resource.

Gates are unitary operations. Single-qubit gates plus one two-qubit entangler (CNOT/CZ) are universal. Clifford gates are easy to simulate classically. Non-Clifford gates (T, Toffoli) make circuits hard to simulate and dominate fault-tolerant cost.

Why error correction is the central problem. Physical gates fail roughly 1 in 10² to 1 in 10⁴ operations, but useful algorithms need about 10⁹–10¹² reliable ones. Quantum error correction (QEC) encodes one logical qubit in many physical qubits and repeatedly measures error syndromes. It works only when physical error is below a code-dependent threshold (about 1% for the surface code). Better metrics than raw qubit count: two-qubit gate fidelity, coherence time, connectivity, gate speed, and logical error rate per cycle.

3 · State of play by company

Google Quantum AI

Superconducting
  • Willow (105 qubits, Dec 2024, Nature): surface-code logical error fell by Λ≈2.14 per distance step (3→5→7). At d=7 (101 physical qubits): 0.143%/cycle, and the logical qubit outlived the best physical qubit by 2.4×. primary
  • Quantum Echoes (OTOC, Nature, 22 Oct 2025): claimed first verifiable advantage, ~13,000× faster than the best classical algorithm for that task. It is a physics benchmark, not a commercially useful computation. vendor claim
  • Mar 2026: set a 2029 deadline for its own PQC migration. primary
  • Mar 2026: hired Adam Kaufman (JILA) to lead a neutral-atom program. secondary
  • No new 2026 fault-tolerance date found.

IBM

Superconducting
  • Nov 2025: Nighthawk (120 qubits, 218 tunable couplers, up to 5,000 two-qubit gates, targeting 7,500 in 2026 and 15,000 on 1,000+ qubits by 2028) and Loon (all hardware elements for fault tolerance). Real-time qLDPC decoding was 10× faster than the prior approach. primary
  • Jun 2026: committed >$10B over five years. primary
  • Jul 30 2026, with UChicago: 70 logical qubits, 2,415 logical 2Q gates, 468 logical T gates in a verifiable sampling task. IBM: "firmly in the quantum advantage era." A sampling benchmark; spoofing rebuttals have historically followed such claims. vendor claim
  • Aug 2026: two cryogenic modules joined and cooled in one environment.
  • Roadmap: Starling 2029 (~200 logical qubits, 100M logical gates); Blue Jay 2033+ (~2,000 logical qubits, 1B gates).

Quantinuum

Trapped ion · Nasdaq: QNT
  • Helios (commercial Nov 2025): 98 barium-137 qubits, QCCD. 2Q fidelity ≈99.92%, 1Q 99.9975%. Reported 48 error-corrected (~2:1 encoding) and 94 error-detected logical qubits. secondary
  • Sep 2026: "Helix" fault-tolerant architecture shown on Helios. secondary
  • Oct 19 2026: 99.9995% logical fidelity (≈5 errors per million operations, color code), 10× better than its own Sep result. Claims 100× better than Google's published result. This is a logical-memory primitive, not a full algorithm at scale, and the comparison is vendor-framed. vendor claim
  • Roadmap: Sol 2027; Apollo (universal FT) 2029; Lumos (>1M physical qubits) early 2030s.

IonQ

Trapped ion · NYSE: IONQ
  • Oct 2025: 99.99% two-qubit fidelity using Oxford Ionics-derived electronic qubit control (laser-free). primary
  • Aug 2026: closed the SkyWater (chip foundry) acquisition. Q2 revenue +287% y/y; 2026 guidance $280–290M. Much of the growth is acquisition-driven (organic/inorganic split not verified). primary
  • Sep 2026: Superion 256 (256-qubit chip fabbed at SkyWater); customer deliveries in 2027.
  • Earlier claim of ~2M physical / 80,000 logical qubits by 2030 was not re-verified. Treat as aspirational. vendor claim

Microsoft (+ Atom Computing)

Topological / neutral atom
  • Majorana 1 (Feb 2025): a Nature peer-review note said the data were not evidence for Majorana zero modes. In Jun 2026 Nature published a formal critique arguing trivial explanations. Microsoft disputes it. disputed
  • Majorana 2 (Jun 2026): claims 1,000× better reliability, ~20 s mean "lifetime", and a scalable machine by 2029. The lifetime is a Microsoft-defined parity-type metric, not a gate fidelity or logical qubit. No independent replication found. vendor claim
  • Magne (Denmark, with Atom Computing): ~1,225 neutral-atom qubits targeting 50 logical, €80M funded, operational ~early 2027. Under construction, not delivered. secondary
  • Nov 2024: 24 entangled logical qubits (Microsoft/Atom). primary

PsiQuantum

Photonic
  • $1B Series E in 2025 at ~$7B valuation (>$3B total raised). secondary
  • Building utility-scale sites in Chicago and Brisbane (Moreton Bay Central); chips at GlobalFoundries. Goal: ~1M-qubit-class fault-tolerant machine.
  • No fault-tolerant demonstration yet. Targets of about 2027–2029 are aspirational.

QuEra / Harvard / MIT

Neutral atom
  • Sep 2025 (Nature): a 3,000-qubit array ran continuously for hours with atom replenishment.
  • Nov 2025 (Nature): 448-atom fault-tolerant architecture, errors detected and removed below a threshold.
  • QuEra raised >$230M (Dec 2025). primary
  • A reported Jan 2026 "~96 logical qubits" result appears in one aggregator only and is unverified; not relied on here.

Infleqtion

Neutral atom
  • Sep 24 2026: 30 logical qubits entangled from 80 physical atoms. Trade press notes it relies on error detection, not full correction. vendor claim

Rigetti

Superconducting
  • 108-qubit Cepheus-1-108Q was delayed in Jan 2026, then generally available Apr 7 2026 (Rigetti QCS, Amazon Braket). primary
  • Small-scale; no QEC milestone.

D-Wave

Annealing → gate model
  • Mar 2025 (Science): claimed "quantum supremacy" on a materials-simulation problem. Classical tensor-network rebuttals attempted to match it. contested
  • Jan 2026: bought Quantum Circuits (dual-rail, gate-model) for $550M. Target: 100 logical qubits by end-2032. secondary
  • Q1 2026 revenue $2.9M (−81% y/y), record bookings $33.4M driven mainly by a $20M sale to Florida Atlantic University. Shows how lumpy and small real quantum revenue is.

DARPA QBI

Government validation
  • Tests whether utility-scale quantum computing (value > cost) is achievable by 2033.
  • Stage B selected 11 companies (Nov 2025). Oct 7 2026: Stage C adds Atom Computing, Diraq, IBM and IonQ, joining Microsoft and PsiQuantum. primary
  • Quantinuum, Google and QuEra Stage C status not confirmed; trade press mentions six "final test" participants, not reconciled.
  • DARPA: "we increasingly expect that someone will build a utility-scale quantum computer by 2033." Evaluating, not guaranteeing.

Honest read. Logical fidelities near 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁶ per operation on small codes have been shown. Not yet shown: the ~100–1,000+ logical qubits at ~10⁻⁹–10⁻¹² error for ≥10⁸–10⁹ gates that Shor on RSA/ECC or industrially valuable chemistry needs. "Logical qubit" counts are not comparable across vendors: codes, error detection vs correction, post-selection, and encoding overhead (2:1 vs hundreds:1) all differ.

Synthesis (author's judgment). Many credible players cluster on 2029 for first small-to-mid-scale fault-tolerant machines, with hundreds of logical qubits at best and likely too small for RSA-2048. Cryptographically relevant machines are plausibly in the early-to-mid 2030s, with wide uncertainty. Timelines pulled in during 2025–26, but roadmap dates have historically slipped (Rigetti's 108-qubit delay in Jan 2026). Treat 2029 as "target if everything works," not a forecast.

7 · Post-quantum cryptography: the part that is already real

Standards

  • 13 Aug 2024: NIST finalized FIPS 203 (ML-KEM), FIPS 204 (ML-DSA), FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA).
  • HQC selected Mar 2025 as backup KEM (draft expected).
  • FIPS 206 (FN-DSA/Falcon) was still pending per sources found. Verify current status at csrc.nist.gov.
  • NIST IR 8547 (draft): deprecate RSA/ECC at 112-bit security after 2030, disallow after 2035.

Deployment

  • Hybrid X25519MLKEM768 is on by default in major browsers. Cloudflare reported over two-thirds (~70%) of human browser traffic post-quantum protected in 2026 (secondary sources).
  • This covers key exchange, which answers harvest-now-decrypt-later. Certificates and signatures lag.
  • Google moved its own migration deadline to 2029 (Mar 25 2026), six years ahead of NIST's 2035 backstop.
  • AES-256 and hashes are considered fine against known quantum attacks.
  • Bitcoin/Ethereum signatures are ECDLP-based, so Google's Mar 2026 paper adds urgency. No cryptographically relevant quantum computer exists today.

8 · Hype vs reality

Read this before repeating any quantum marketing line.
  • "Advantage" claims keep being contested. Random circuit sampling, OTOC and D-Wave annealing results all drew classical-simulation counters. IBM now runs a public Quantum Advantage Tracker to adjudicate. Advantages shown so far are on contrived or physics-benchmark tasks.
  • No commercially useful advantage has been conclusively demonstrated for a real business problem (our conclusion from the sources; vendors assert otherwise for narrow cases).
  • Logical-qubit counts are slippery: detection vs correction, post-selection, code distance and overhead differ.
  • Revenue is small: D-Wave Q1 2026 revenue was $2.9M; IonQ's growth is largely acquisitions (SkyWater, Oxford Ionics). Valuations are far above revenue and equities are volatile.
  • Data loading bottleneck: quantum computers are poor at big-data problems, which matters for any "quantum for AI/ML" pitch.
  • Roadmap risk: delays are common; 2029 assumes cryogenics, wiring, decoders, yield and manufacturing scale on plan.
  • Not a general accelerator: no known exponential speedup for most optimization, NP-complete problems or deep-learning training.
  • Source quality: much 2026 coverage is press-release echo or aggregator content; prefer the primary sources below.
Open items we could not resolve: Quantinuum Apollo/Sol details, Google's current FT roadmap and IonQ's 2030 targets were not re-verified; QuEra's "96 logical qubits" is aggregator-only; the full DARPA Stage C roster is unconfirmed; FIPS 206 and HQC draft status need re-checking.
