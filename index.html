In 60 seconds

Is there a relationship? Yes, but it is asymmetric, and narrower than the hype suggests.

  • ASI does not need quantum. No credible roadmap to AGI/ASI requires a quantum computer, and there is no evidence that general intelligence requires quantum physics. Established
  • AI helps quantum far more than quantum helps AI today. AI already works on error-correction decoding, code and circuit discovery, and compilation. Quantum speedups for machine learning remain mostly theoretical, and classical "dequantization" removed several headline cases. Mixed: some peer-reviewed, much is preprint or vendor
  • The real overlap is security. Shor's algorithm threatens RSA and elliptic-curve cryptography whether or not AI exists. AI mainly changes the tempo of attack and defence. NIST post-quantum standards are final. Established
  • The other overlap is hybrid HPC. GPUs are becoming the control, decoding and simulation layer of quantum computers (NVIDIA NVQLink, CUDA-Q). Those are vendor-announced products. Vendor claim
  • Hype to discount: "quantum will train LLMs or deliver AGI", and "quantum will fix AI's energy bill". Unsupported

Key takeaways

Takeaway 1

Decoupled by default

Frontier AI progresses on classical silicon. Quantum matures on its own timeline: IBM, Quantinuum and Google claim fault tolerance around 2029–2033. The research calls decoupling the most likely near-term scenario.

Takeaway 2

AI is quantum's best tool

The strongest evidence points from AI to quantum. Examples: AlphaQubit decoding, and IBM's LLM-guided search that found 465 candidate error-correcting codes (IBM says their practical value is still to be investigated). Many 2026 results are preprints or vendor reports.

Takeaway 3

Act on security, not on hype

The actionable overlap is cryptography. Finish post-quantum migration, build crypto-agility, and treat long-lived secrets as already exposed to harvest-now-decrypt-later. Migration complexity, not cryptanalysis, is the bottleneck for most organizations.

01 · Superintelligence

Artificial superintelligence: hypothetical, but being actively forecast

Where the field stands as of October 2026: what ANI, AGI and ASI actually mean, who is claiming what and when, the physical limits of scaling, and how regulators are responding. Each claim is tagged as fact, claim or speculation.

From narrow AI to superintelligence

ANI

Narrow AI

Systems that perform specific tasks: chess, translation, protein folding. Everything deployed historically.

Fact
AGI

Artificial General Intelligence

Human-level cognition across most tasks. There is no agreed test and definitions differ by lab; Google DeepMind has published a levels-of-AGI framework (breadth × depth × autonomy). Not achieved by consensus. The International AI Safety Report 2026 calls frontier capability "jagged": strong at expert-level maths and code, weak at some simple tasks.

Not achieved
ASI

Artificial Superintelligence

Bostrom (2014): "any intellect that greatly exceeds the cognitive performance of humans in virtually all domains of interest." It does not exist.

Speculative

Forecasters use operational definitions too: AI Impacts' "high-level machine intelligence" (unaided machines outperform humans at every task, more cheaply); AI Futures Project's "Automated Coder" → superhuman coder → ASI milestones. OpenAI describes itself as "a superintelligence research company".

Bostrom's three forms of superintelligence

⚡

A human-like mind running much faster.

🧩

Collective

Many minds aggregated into a larger whole.

🧠

A mind that is qualitatively smarter, not just faster or more numerous.

Amodei's "country of geniuses in a datacenter" is essentially a speed + collective scenario in Bostrom's terms.

Can scaling continue to 2030?

Epoch finds ~2e29 FLOP training runs by 2030 feasible (GPT-4 ≈ 2e25 FLOP). Binding constraints: power first, then chip production. More than 100 GW of AI capacity worldwide (>50 GW in the US, ~5% of US generation capacity) is plausible by 2030. Whether spending continues is speculation.

Epoch AI; Epoch/EPRI · Extrapolation

Reasoning models & agents

  • Inference-time scaling drove "particularly large" gains in maths, software engineering and science over the past year (International AI Safety Report 2026).
  • OpenAI reports gold-medal-level performance at the IMO and IOI (2025). This is OpenAI's own evaluation, not an IMO-coordinated certification at the time.
  • METR time horizon (task length an AI completes at 50% reliability): doubling roughly every ~7 months over ~6 years, ~4–5 months since 2024. METR's Time Horizon 1.1 (29 Jan 2026) measured o3 at ≈ 2 hours (116–119 min).
  • Agents still struggle to recover from basic errors in long workflows.

Infrastructure build-out

OpenAI's "Stargate" is a multi-site build-out, for example a 1 GW campus in Michigan (Oracle / Related Digital) and an SB Energy partnership (9 Jan 2026). The International AI Safety Report notes developers have announced hundreds of billions of dollars of data-centre investment.

Fact · progress through 2030 remains uncertain; trends are consistent with continued improvement.

The central technical disagreement. Altman and Amodei hold that the current trajectory suffices. Sutskever argues pure scaling is hitting limits and that new ideas (better generalization, sample efficiency) are needed. This is unresolved. Energy and chips are the physical bottleneck, which is the natural bridge to the quantum-computing sections: whether energy-efficient or fundamentally different compute can help is open and speculative.

Transformer LLMs with inference-time compute. The dominant strategy at OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind.

Risks and alignment

Three risk families

The International AI Safety Report 2026 groups risks into misuse, malfunctions and systemic disruption. It recommends "defence-in-depth" (layered safeguards), notes open-weight models cannot be recalled and have easily removed safeguards, and urges societal resilience measures.

Fact

Core ASI arguments

Bostrom's control problem and Hinton's point that we have "never created beings smarter than us": loss of control, power concentration, bio and cyber misuse. Hinton warns AI could design viruses or cyberattacks and cause "chaos just by talking to people".

Speculative / claim

Amodei's categories

  • Autonomous AI takeover
  • Misuse by bad actors (e.g. terrorists with "mercenary" AI)
  • Economic disruption and mass unemployment
  • Indirect destabilization
Claim

Alignment research directions

Scalable oversight, interpretability, evaluations (METR, UK AISI), red-teaming, control and monitoring. These are described only generally in the Safety Report; specific lab methods were not individually verified.

Governance and regulation

🇪🇺 EU AI Act

Regulation 2024/1689. Prohibited practices (Feb 2025) and general-purpose AI model rules (Aug 2025) apply. Commission enforcement powers over GPAI and Art. 50 transparency obligations apply from 2 Aug 2026.

The Digital Omnibus on AI (Reg. 2026/1744, in force 27 Jul 2026) delayed high-risk obligations to 2 Dec 2027 (Annex III) and 2 Aug 2028 (Annex I). Art. 50 breaches carry fines up to €15M / 3%.

🇺🇸 US federal

EO 14179 (23 Jan 2025) removed the Biden-era AI order. EO 14365 (11 Dec 2025, "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for AI") sets up a DOJ AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state AI laws and conditions some BEAD funds. It cannot itself invalidate state laws.

California SB 53

Transparency in Frontier AI Act, signed 29 Sep 2025: the first US state law expressly regulating frontier developers. Disclosure / safety-framework and incident-reporting design; successor to the vetoed SB 1047. The legal challenge and preemption fight is ongoing and unresolved.

International

The International AI Safety Report (Bengio-led, UK-hosted, 2nd edition Feb 2026; 100+ experts, 30+ countries) informs policy but is not binding. As far as this research found, no binding international regime on superintelligence exists.

Civil society

The FLI prohibition statement (Oct 2025) is the most visible call to stop superintelligence development outright.

Direction of travel

Governance is fragmenting: the EU is delaying high-risk rules, the US federal government is pushing preemption against state frontier-AI laws, and some groups want outright prohibition.

Not covered: Chinese governance, UN processes, and GPAI-model fine levels under the EU Act (not verified).

What we know vs. what we speculate

Known

  • ASI does not exist; AGI is not achieved by consensus.
  • Frontier training compute grows ~4–5×/yr; data-centre electricity is projected to pass 1,000 TWh by 2030 (IEA).
  • Reasoning models have made real gains; capability stays jagged.
  • Regulation exists (EU AI Act, California SB 53) but is unsettled and fragmenting.

Speculative or contested

  • When, or whether, AGI and ASI arrive. Forecasts range from 2027 to 2047+.
  • Whether scaling alone suffices, or new research paradigms are needed.
  • Extinction probabilities. They are subjective and even Hinton says nobody knows.
  • Whether spending, power and chips keep up to 2030.
  • Any role for quantum computing in reaching ASI.

1 · The fundamentals, in plain English

Qubit & superposition

A qubit is a two-level quantum system. Its state is a|0⟩ + b|1⟩, and measuring it returns 0 or 1 with probabilities |a|² and |b|². n qubits carry 2ⁿ amplitudes.

This is not "trying every answer at once." You read out only n classical bits. Useful algorithms use interference to boost the amplitudes of correct answers, so any speedup comes from structure in the problem.

Entanglement & gates

Entangled states can't be written as a product of single-qubit states. They give correlations with no classical analogue, and algorithms consume them as a resource.

Gates are unitary operations. Single-qubit gates plus one two-qubit entangler (CNOT/CZ) are universal. Clifford gates are easy to simulate classically. Non-Clifford gates (T, Toffoli) make circuits hard to simulate and dominate fault-tolerant cost.

HXZST●─⊕

Schematic illustration, not to scale. Bloch sphere (one qubit) · Bell-pair circuit · physical qubits encoding one logical qubit.

Why error correction is the central problem. Physical gates fail roughly 1 in 10² to 1 in 10⁴ operations, but useful algorithms need about 10⁹–10¹² reliable ones. Quantum error correction (QEC) encodes one logical qubit in many physical qubits and repeatedly measures error syndromes. It works only when physical error is below a code-dependent threshold (about 1% for the surface code). Better metrics than raw qubit count: two-qubit gate fidelity, coherence time, connectivity, gate speed, and logical error rate per cycle.

  • NIST IR 8547 (draft): deprecate RSA/ECC at 112-bit security after 2030, disallow after 2035.

Deployment

  • Hybrid X25519MLKEM768 is on by default in major browsers. Cloudflare reported over two-thirds (~70%) of human browser traffic post-quantum protected in 2026 (secondary sources).
  • This covers key exchange, which answers harvest-now-decrypt-later. Certificates and signatures lag.
  • Google moved its own migration deadline to 2029 (Mar 25 2026), six years ahead of NIST's 2035 backstop.
  • AES-256 and hashes are considered fine against known quantum attacks.
  • Bitcoin/Ethereum signatures are ECDLP-based, so Google's Mar 2026 paper adds urgency. No cryptographically relevant quantum computer exists today.

8 · Hype vs reality

Read this before repeating any quantum marketing line.
  • "Advantage" claims keep being contested. Random circuit sampling, OTOC and D-Wave annealing results all drew classical-simulation counters. IBM now runs a public Quantum Advantage Tracker to adjudicate. Advantages shown so far are on contrived or physics-benchmark tasks.
  • No commercially useful advantage has been conclusively demonstrated for a real business problem (our conclusion from the sources; vendors assert otherwise for narrow cases).
  • Logical-qubit counts are slippery: detection vs correction, post-selection, code distance and overhead differ.
  • Revenue is small: D-Wave Q1 2026 revenue was $2.9M; IonQ's growth is largely acquisitions (SkyWater, Oxford Ionics). Valuations are far above revenue and equities are volatile.
  • Data loading bottleneck: quantum computers are poor at big-data problems, which matters for any "quantum for AI/ML" pitch.
  • Roadmap risk: delays are common; 2029 assumes cryogenics, wiring, decoders, yield and manufacturing scale on plan.
  • Not a general accelerator: no known exponential speedup for most optimization, NP-complete problems or deep-learning training.
  • Source quality: much 2026 coverage is press-release echo or aggregator content; prefer the primary sources below.
Open items we could not resolve: Quantinuum Apollo/Sol details, Google's current FT roadmap and IonQ's 2030 targets were not re-verified; QuEra's "96 logical qubits" is aggregator-only; the full DARPA Stage C roster is unconfirmed; FIPS 206 and HQC draft status need re-checking.

03 · The relationship

Is there a relationship? Yes, but it is asymmetric.

AI is already helping build quantum computers. Quantum computing is not helping build superintelligence, and nothing in any credible roadmap to AGI/ASI requires it. The most concrete shared ground is plumbing (GPUs) and security (cryptography).

Superintelligence ⇏ needs quantumEvery frontier AI system runs on classical silicon (GPUs/TPUs).
Traffic runs mostly AI → quantumDecoding, code discovery, circuit optimization, calibration.
The hard link is securityShor threatens RSA/ECC with or without AI; AI changes the tempo.
Infrastructure is sharedGPUs are becoming the control, decoding and simulation layer of quantum machines.

Quantum → AI weak link

Would quantum computers make AI smarter or cheaper? Theory says: rarely, and not for the workloads that matter most.

Quantum machine learning headline claims fell

HHL, quantum PCA, quantum recommendation systems and quantum SVMs were advertised as exponentially faster than classical ML.

  • Dequantization. In 2018 Ewin Tang gave a classical algorithm matching the Kerenidis–Prakash recommendation algorithm up to a polynomial slowdown. Follow-ups dequantized PCA, low-rank regression and supervised clustering. The exponential gap came from the state-preparation assumption.
  • Scope. This hits low-rank, well-conditioned linear-algebra QML. It does not refute quantum advantage for all ML.
  • Power of data (Huang et al., 2021): with enough training data, classical models match quantum models on many datasets.
  • Variational/NISQ QML suffers barren plateaus; provable absence of them often implies classical simulability. No credible evidence that today's quantum neural nets beat classical ones.

One "hybrid chemistry net" accuracy gain exists only as a secondary report, unverified in the primary preprint, so we do not rely on it.

The QRAM / data-loading wall well established

Loading N classical numbers into amplitudes costs O(N) time, or a QRAM whose hardware, energy and error-correction overhead is itself roughly O(N) physical resources. A 2026 paper still calls data loading "a fundamental headwind relative to classical methods".

Aaronson's "Read the fine print" lists the input, output, conditioning and classical-baseline caveats. For billions of tokens, just reading the data once already dominates.

LLM training: quantum linear-algebra primitives (HHL, QSVT) need QRAM, tolerance of high condition numbers and full-vector readout. These assumptions mostly fail for transformer training. No credible path to quantum-accelerated frontier training exists today.

Where a quantum learning edge is proven proven

When the data itself is quantum. Huang et al. (Science, 2022) proved an exponential advantage for a quantum learner with quantum memory on certain physical-property-learning tasks.

That is learning about quantum physics, not training language models. Quantum simulation giving AI better chemistry/materials data once fault tolerance arrives is plausible; generic LLM acceleration is not supported by theory.

Sampling and optimization: beyond-classical sampling demos (random circuit sampling) do not yield useful ML models. QAOA/annealing has no broadly accepted end-to-end speedup on practical instances; treat vendor "optimization advantage" claims as unproven.

Penrose–Hameroff: is quantum needed for AGI? unsupported

Orch-OR claims cognition depends on gravity-induced collapse in microtubules and is non-computable, so classical AGI would be impossible.

  • Tegmark's decoherence estimate (2000) says coherence times are far too short. Hameroff et al. (2002) dispute the parameters, and no decisive experiment has settled it.
  • Penrose's Gödel-based argument is widely regarded as flawed by logicians.
  • Classical LLM scaling already reached olympiad-level maths and coding with no quantum component.

Verdict: no evidence that quantum physics is required for general intelligence. Even if brains used it, silicon intelligence need not copy brains.

AI → Quantum strong link

The direction with the strongest, most concrete evidence. Much of the 2026 wave is preprints and vendor reports, and is labelled as such.

AlphaQubit: ML error decoding peer-reviewed

Google DeepMind + Google Quantum AI (Nature, Nov 2024): a recurrent-transformer decoder for the surface code. It beat other state-of-the-art decoders on real Sycamore data at distance 3 and 5, stayed ahead in simulation up to distance 11, and generalized from 25 training rounds to simulated 100,000 rounds.

Honest limit (Google's own): still too slow for real-time correction on a superconducting chip (checks happen ~1 million times per second). Training at millions of qubits needs more data-efficient methods.

Competitor: IBM's Nov 2025 claim of real-time-capable classical (non-AI) decoding at ~10× speedup is vendor-reported. Classical decoders may be the realistic real-time path.

AI discovery of codes, circuits, encodings promising · mostly preprints

  • Harvard/QuEra (Oct 2026, preprint): LLM-in-the-loop evolutionary search with an independent verifier found circuits with fewer Toffoli gates and qubits than state of the art in 7 of 8 tasks, plus a new reversible modular-inversion architecture.
  • IBM (Jun 2026, vendor): LLM-guided search found 465 candidate QEC codes; IBM says the practical potential of the top candidates is still to be investigated.
  • qBraid + AlphaEvolve (Jun 2026, vendor/secondary preprint): claimed fermion-to-qubit encodings at distance 5 (previous known: 3), with 4–5× fewer data qubits for fault-tolerant chemistry. Unreviewed.
  • Phasecraft (Oct 2026): AI agents for circuit optimization. Preprint, abstract only.

Compilation & calibration widely published

Reinforcement-learning agents have been applied to compiling for neutral-atom move synthesis and trapped-ion circuits (arXiv preprints).

ML-based qubit tuning and pulse optimization is widely published and being productized. Specific 2026 calibration results were not verified for this guide, so none are cited.

Chemistry & materials: AI as rival and partner mixed

Simulating strongly correlated electrons is the flagship quantum application, and also where AI competes: neural-network potentials and neural quantum states keep raising the classical bar. Hybrid "AI + quantum" drug-discovery pipelines are mostly roadmaps.

Google's Quantum Echoes (Nature, Oct 2025) is the best case for verifiable quantum advantage. Google claims 13,000× over a leading supercomputer on a benchmark. The NMR application was proof-of-principle only, not yet beyond-classical for a useful molecule.

Shared infrastructure: it's about plumbing

The GPU is the infrastructure of AI and also the infrastructure around quantum. For the foreseeable future, quantum computers are accelerators attached to classical AI-era supercomputers, not replacements for them.

NVQLink vendor

NVIDIA's open architecture (announced 28 Oct 2025) linking QPU controllers to GPU supercomputers for real-time QEC and control. It lists 17 QPU builders, 5 controller builders and 9 US national labs. A Qblox integration reports round-trip latency under 4 µs on an RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell.

CUDA-Q vendor

CUDA-Q Logical (14 Sep 2026) is an orchestration layer for fault-tolerant application design. Fermilab reports a 7× design-cycle speedup (5 months to 3 weeks). NVIDIA/Jülich announced a 104-qubit magic-state-cultivation simulation on JUPITER.

Energy & compute "quantum fixes it" = hype

IEA: data centres used ~415 TWh in 2024; hyperscaler capex exceeded $400B in 2025, with a further ~75% rise expected in 2026. Quantum machines need cryogenics, control electronics and large classical decoding stacks, and no known quantum algorithm replaces the bulk of transformer compute.

Hardware reality check (Oct 2026). Google demonstrated magic-state cultivation on Willow with ~40× error reduction (Science), still a small-scale demo. Quantinuum reports 99.9995% logical fidelity on Helios and targets fault-tolerant "Apollo" by 2029 (vendor). IBM targets Starling in 2029 (vendor). A 2026 Nature critique questions Microsoft's Majorana 1 claims. Fault-tolerant machines at useful scale do not yet exist.

Shor & harvest-now-decrypt-later

Shor's algorithm breaks RSA, DH and elliptic-curve crypto. Symmetric crypto and hashes are only quadratically weakened by Grover, and AES-256 stays OK.

HNDL: adversaries can store encrypted traffic today and decrypt it later. It matters for long-lived secrets (state, health, IP). Public evidence of specific large-scale harvesting is mostly inference by agencies and commentators.

PQC response

NIST finalized FIPS 203 (ML-KEM), 204 (ML-DSA) and 205 (SLH-DSA) in Aug 2024. NSA's CNSA 2.0 requires new national-security-system acquisitions to be quantum-resistant from 2027.

NIST IR 8547 transition dates (draft recalled as deprecate by 2030, disallow by 2035) are not verified against the final text. Check before relying on them.

How AI enters the picture

  • Cyber capability needs no quantum. A Cloud Security Alliance note reports a lab rating its model "Critical" for cyber, with self-reported benchmark scores the CSA stresses are unreplicated. Independent preprints show the same direction. Treat as vendor-reported.
  • Possible speed-up of the quantum threat. The Oct 2026 LLM-circuit paper targets modular inversion and number-theory primitives, the building blocks of factoring and elliptic-curve attacks. No AI-found improvement was found that changes Gidney's headline numbers.
  • Defence benefits too: crypto-inventory, migration tooling and PQC implementation verification.

Not a "break everything" scenario

AI does not break mathematics. A lattice or hash break would need new mathematical results; a superintelligence might find them, but that is speculative either way. The bottleneck for most organizations is migration complexity, not cryptanalysis.

Convergence scenarios

Most likely near-term

1 · Decoupled

ASI-class AI advances on GPUs; quantum matures on its own timeline (fault tolerance claimed ~2029–2033 by IBM, Quantinuum and Google). They meet only in infrastructure and security.

Plausible · starting

2 · AI accelerates quantum

AI-found codes, circuits, decoders and compilers shrink qubit and gate counts; agents speed up hardware design and calibration. Could pull forward cryptographically relevant machines by an unknown amount (possibly years), giving a faster, less predictable Q-day than survey data suggests.

Speculative

3 · Quantum accelerates AI

Needs fault-tolerant machines plus a real advantage on a core AI workload. Specialized scientific-AI benefits (quantum-simulated chemistry data, learning from quantum experiments) are plausible; generic LLM/AGI acceleration is not supported by current theory.

Tail risk · our label

4 · Compound cyber risk

Strong autonomous cyber-capable AI plus a cryptographically relevant quantum computer in the same window would collapse trust in legacy PKI and make HNDL archives suddenly readable. The mitigation is dull and available: finish PQC migration, build crypto-agility, and treat long-lived secrets as already exposed.

The likelihood label is our judgment; the research gives no probability for this combination.

Two meta-risks

  • Concentration: frontier AI labs (Google, IBM, Microsoft, NVIDIA ecosystem) are also leading quantum players, so AI-for-quantum feedback loops accrue to a handful of firms and states.
  • Hype: "Quantum-AI" is a marketing category; funding and valuations can detach from technical evidence (see QML and the Majorana controversy).

Cryptographic

The scarce skills are workflow integration, evaluation, data engineering and governance.

What to do now: action playbook

PQC migration is the highest-urgency track; it is backed by federal deadlines and does not depend on quantum hardware arriving early.

1. Post-quantum cryptography Highest urgency

  1. Inventory cryptography (CBOM) across apps, devices, vendors and certificates.
  2. Prioritise by data shelf-life (harvest-now-decrypt-later): long-lived confidential data first; key establishment (ML-KEM) before signatures (ML-DSA).
  3. Write it into contracts: require vendor PQC delivery dates; check FIPS 140-3 validation.
  4. Align to the earliest applicable deadline: federal high-value assets and contractors 2030–31; other civilian systems 2035; EU/France regulators earlier.
  5. Build crypto-agility (hybrid classical + PQC modes) so algorithms can be swapped.

2. Quantum readiness Start small

  1. Appoint an owner and a 12-month plan. Map exposure: optimisation-heavy, simulation-heavy or crypto-heavy. Split strategy into what can wait vs what must start now (Gartner frame).
  2. Run 1–3 scoped pilots on QCaaS/hybrid solvers with an explicit classical baseline and kill criteria. Co-development is becoming the standard model (McKinsey).
  3. Start small. One-third of large companies analysed spent over $10M in 2025; most don't need that. A small team plus cloud credits is enough to begin.
  4. Build HPC–QC integration skills and keep algorithm IP in-house.
  5. Watch fault-tolerance milestones, not qubit counts: IBM 2029, Quantinuum Sol 2027 / Apollo 2029, Google.

3. AI adoption Now

  1. Tie each use case to a P&L metric and redesign the workflow, not just add a copilot.
  2. Embedded AI first for quick wins; custom/agentic systems only where you hold a data advantage.
  3. Governance and cost control: usage tracking, model portability, data sovereignty.
  4. Scenario-plan for much more capable AI (staffing, security, partner concentration) without committing capital to speculative timelines.
  5. Don't be the bag-holder of AI infrastructure: rent capacity and keep multi-vendor options; impairment risk sits with providers, but price shocks and discontinuations reach customers.
The deadlines. US Executive Order 'Securing the Nation Against Advanced Cryptographic Attacks' (signed 22 Jun 2026; 91 FR 38483) requires federal high-value assets to use PQC for key establishment by 31 Dec 2030 and signatures by 31 Dec 2031, with a FAR rule for contractors proposed by 19 Dec 2026. Other civilian systems: 2035 (OMB M-26-15). NIST IR 8547's 2030/2035 dates are still draft. Sources disagree on the order's number, so cite it by title and date. Gartner: fewer than half of CISOs have started PQC, and only 24% of those show measurable progress. Verdict

The bottom line

Superintelligence and quantum computing are two separate technology bets that touch in a few real places. They are not one thing.

1. AI is the present; superintelligence is a scenario.

AI spending is forecast at $2.67T in 2026 (Gartner), yet only 39% of firms report enterprise-level EBIT impact. AGI has no agreed definition or date, so plan with scenarios, not dates.

2. Quantum is early-commercial, not yet useful at scale.

Revenue is above $1B (2025, McKinsey), and pilots like HSBC and Ford Otosan are real but narrow. Gartner puts production-grade fault tolerance 5–10 years out.

3. The real intersection is hybrid workflows and security.

AI + HPC + QPU pipelines, AI for quantum error correction, and PQC as the common denominator. We found no consensus evidence that superintelligence needs quantum hardware, and no demonstrated quantum advantage on commercial ML.

4. Act now on cryptography; stay disciplined on the rest.

PQC migration is the one high-certainty action, with US deadlines in 2030 and 2031. Pilot quantum small with kill criteria, tie AI to P&L, and treat valuations in both fields with caution (Gartner flags over $3.5T of AI infrastructure at impairment risk).

What would change this view

Towards faster / bigger

  • Peer-reviewed, commercially priced quantum advantage on a business workload, with fair classical baselines.
  • Fault-tolerance milestones met on schedule (IBM Starling 2029, Quantinuum Apollo 2029).
  • Credible evidence that quantum methods help train or run frontier AI.
  • Audited production savings, not trials, from quantum pilots.

Towards slower / smaller

  • Classical algorithms closing the gap on claimed quantum advantages.
  • AI revenue falling short of capex, triggering the impairments Gartner warns about.
  • Enterprise AI returns staying concentrated rather than broadening.
  • Evidence of a cryptographically relevant quantum machine (pulls PQC deadlines forward), or a long stall in error correction (pushes them back).
